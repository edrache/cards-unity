The entrance journey

ProceduralCave now begins with an automatic walk through a long entrance tunnel while Story plays the editable narrative. The torch is lit and animates normally throughout. The original Blackout / Image remains disabled; the camera child CameraBlackout (formerly Hide) supplies the world-space veil. Its collider and shadows are disabled so the presentation cannot obstruct movement or light probes. The player occlusion outline is suppressed until the veil is fully dissolved, then restored.

Edit Assets/Settings/Gameplay/Cave Intro.asset for narrative text and timing. Each newline is a separate beat, long beats wrap, and TMP rich text such as <b>These are their stories.</b> is supported. Characters Per Second controls cadence, Character Fade Duration controls each letter's alpha, Line Hold Duration starts after the complete reveal, and Line Fade Duration separates beats. Pressing a keyboard, mouse or controller button, or engaging a stick, accelerates the current beat while the intro owns movement. The final beat plays automatically while the player can move. The Grenze font retains CaveUI fallback coverage for Polish characters.

ProceduralCave Generation / Settings / Entrance Length is 150 m in this scene; the supported range is 8–160 m. The winding route to the first room's centre is longer. Rebuild after editing the profile. The generated entrance route starts at the player spawn, follows the tunnel centre and ends at the actual entrance chamber. It is excluded from rockfall traps and reserves extra centre clearance from scattered rocks. Increasing length expands the cave mesh sampling area and can increase rebuild time.

Story Intro Controller on Story Intro Canvas binds Cave, Player Root, Player Torch, Journey Protection, Veil, Sequence Player and Gameplay UI groups. Auto Walk Speed defaults to 1.15 m/s and uses collision-aware character movement, natural facing and distance-driven gait. Path Look Ahead (0.9 m) controls centreline steering. Manual Approach Distance (18 m) reserves the last stretch before the chamber for the player. If narration lasts longer than the walk, the character waits at that boundary. Fast-skipping leaves a longer manual walk. Simulation and ambient audio continue; this intro does not pause global time.

When the last beat begins, automatic movement ends and player control returns. Held attack/interact inputs must be released before they can act. At the same moment, Transition Duration (2.8 s in this scene) dissolves CameraBlackout with organic noise and brings the HUD back. The material Assets/Materials/StoryVeil.mat exposes Veil Color, Noise Scale, Warm Edge Width and Warm Edge Color. Story Veil Dissolve / SetProgress drives the material through a property block and disables its renderer at full reveal.

Intro Journey Protection on Shadow Knight remains active after control returns. It prevents damage, death, knockdown and stress accumulation; torch time, strike and drop-impact fuel costs are suspended. Only entry into the first generated chamber reached by the player releases protection and starts fuel consumption. If a branch leads to another chamber first, that chamber also ends protection. Returning to the tunnel afterward does not re-enable safety. The entrance contains no room population rules or traps. This protection is per character, not a global invulnerability setting.

Story Sequence Player remains reusable: Begin(), Advance() and Stop() control playback; LineStarted exposes beat changes, IsLastLine identifies the finale and Completed fires after its fade. The controller keeps running until both the narrative and the protected journey are complete. Disabling an active controller cancels the sequence, reveals the view and restores control and saved protection states.

On death, Shadow Knight / Character Death / Death Veil reuses CameraBlackout in reverse: organic noise closes the view over Veil Duration (2.8 seconds). This animation uses unscaled time and finishes even if the defeat summary pauses gameplay first. The player outline is disabled; the defeat summary remains visible above the world veil. Leave Death Veil empty in scenes without this presentation.

Falling cave water

The Falling Water entry in Room Content Rules uses Content Type = Prefabs and Assets/Prefabs/Falling Water.prefab. The prefab's Cave Water component owns its shared material and dimensions: Width 0.8 m, Height 5 m, Depth 0.35 m. Height is twice the original 2.5 m. Edit the prefab to tune all generated streams, or place it directly in a scene. The mesh is generated on enable and does not need a saved mesh asset.

Room Percentage selects rooms; Minimum/Maximum Per Selected Room selects the number of streams. Seed Salt gives the rule its own deterministic random stream. The current rule keeps Align To Floor off and Wall Placement Percentage at zero, so water stays upright and floor-anchored. Surface Offset lifts the entire effect. Footprint Radius is 0.6 m; when widening or scaling the prefab, increase the reserved footprint to cover its stream and ripple area. Wall Clearance and Route Clearance control placement. Water is non-blocking and does not change corridor geometry. Rebuild the cave after editing the rule or prefab.

The effect uses a 6-triangle procedural mesh, animated streaks and floor ripples in a shared shader, without particles, fluid simulation, extra lights or refraction. The DitherAccent pass carries the material colour through the cave renderer; the shared material retains its current Inspector tuning. Opaque rocks still occlude it. Tune the material for appearance. A missing material disables wet contact. The older Content Type = Water remains supported for procedural rules with nested dimensions/material, but the default Falling Water rule now reads its appearance from the prefab.

A flame touching or crossing the falling water is extinguished immediately, including carried, swinging and dropped torches. Contact follows the flame position after the pose overlays, with an 8 cm contact radius and a swept check between consecutive frames. The handle or player entering alone does not extinguish the flame. Fire, smoke and light stop together; the torch stays extinguished after dropping, pickup or disable/re-enable for the rest of that session. The decorative ripple area is not an additional wet hazard. Water does not modify shared torch balance and there is no relighting interaction.

Cave grass

Cave Grass is a seeded visual layer generated as part of the cave rebuild. In Room Content Rules, add/select a rule with Content Type = Grass, then use its Grass Settings. Grass Room Percentage selects rooms (35% on the current profile; default 0), while Grass Blades Per Square Metre (0–48, default 18) sets the requested density. Selected rooms are sampled independently and stratified; blades follow Floor Height and reject walls, rocks, existing room content and treasures. Zero percentage or zero density disables the layer.

Grass Height (0.3–2.5 m, default 1.2), Grass Maximum Blades (0–120,000, default 60,000), Grass Draw Distance (8–80 m, default 32) and Grass Bend Radius (0.3–3 m, default 1.1) control appearance, safety and rendering cost. The maximum is a global safety cap: density is reduced across selected rooms before obstacle rejection, so the final count may be lower. Grass Height Variation (default 0.55) independently varies each blade from 45% to 155% of Grass Height. Grass Width is the full root width in metres (default 0.1); Grass Width Variation (default 0.65) varies it from 35% to 165%. Both variation sliders range from 0 to 0.85; zero disables that variation. Seeded dimensions are generated once, without extra per-frame work. A missing material also disables the layer. Grass is emitted as opaque, double-sided broad blades with short bevelled caps and a gentle lean in 6 m mesh chunks with no per-blade GameObjects, colliders or Update loops. Game view measures grass distance from the player, so zooming the follow camera out does not hide nearby grass. Scene view measures from its camera. Rebuilding while inspecting generated cave content redirects the shared Inspector to the cave root before deleting that content. Distance culling uses a cached profile value, shared with the shader fade distance at rebuild. Distance culling and shrink fade reduce distant cost; the URP material receives torch light but grass casts no shadows.

Grass rules do not need a prefab; prefab-only controls are hidden. Grass executes after all prefab rules, so occupied-area rejection is independent of rule order. Multiple Grass rules are allowed, each with its own Seed Salt and room selection; their budgets sum across rules and overlapping coverage may overlap. Assign a material using Cards Unity/Cave Grass and tune its grass colours there. The vertex shader bends nearby blades around the player with a short trailing wake of about 0.35 seconds. This is a visual effect only: it does not alter movement, enemy exposure, pickup, torch fuel or concealment AI. Rebuild after changing the profile. Only the player creates a wake; there is no persistent physical flattening.

Roots on floors and walls

The enabled Ancient Roots room rule uses three prefabs in Assets/Prefabs/CaveDecorations/: Forked Cave Root, Twisted Cave Root and Cave Root Fan, with the shared Ancient Root Bark material. These are decorative meshes without colliders, so they do not snag the player. The rule requests 1–3 roots in 65% of rooms, with Seed Salt 47022, Footprint Radius 2.2 m and Route Clearance 0.7 m.

Wall Placement Percentage is 55: each requested instance has a 55% chance to try a wall, otherwise it uses the floor. Zero keeps all placements on the floor; 100 tries walls for all instances. A failed wall search falls back to normal floor placement, so the final proportion can differ. Minimum/Maximum Wall Height (0.25–0.75 m) controls attachment height above the local floor, capped below the visible wall top. Wall Embed Depth (0.18 m) hides the origin inside the rock.

Wall placement uses the actual cave field to find a boundary, avoids passage openings and points the prefab’s local +Z into the room. Author the origin at the attachment point. Wall Clearance, Surface Offset, Align To Floor and Random Yaw apply only to floor placements; walls use an upright, inward orientation. Footprint reservations, existing rocks/treasures and Route Clearance still constrain wall placement. Existing rules default to 0% wall placement. Change the rule and choose Rebuild Cave.

Stone pillar decorations

The cave generation profile includes an enabled Stone Pillars entry in Room Content Rules. It chooses between Tall Stone Pillar (about 2.3 m), Broken Stone Pillar (1.1 m) and Twin Stone Pillars (1.9 m), found in Assets/Prefabs/CaveDecorations/. Each prefab has a faceted mesh, the existing Angular Rock material and a solid MeshCollider. These are static obstacles, not collectible items.

The rule requests 1–3 decorations in 40% of rooms, using Seed Salt 47021. Footprint Radius 1.5 m reserves space for all variants, including floor tilt; Wall Clearance 0.45 m and Route Clearance 1.2 m keep them away from walls and corridor centre lines. Align To Floor and Random Yaw are enabled; Surface Offset −0.08 m slightly embeds the base. Crowded rooms may receive fewer. Tune the rule or disable it, then use Rebuild Cave; edit source prefabs rather than generated instances. Adding this rule does not change the seeded cave layout or built-in populations.

Corridor branches and varied lengths

Branch Connection Percentage (0–100, default 0) lets branch tips connect to nearby corridors belonging to other branch families. A family consists of a direct side branch and all its nested descendants. Set 100 to attempt every tip, and use Branch Connection Distance (2–64 m, default 16) to limit the straight-line gap. For example, 100 and 24 m encourages loops between nearby independent branches. Connections snap to the nearest eligible segment, including its interior. Tips already overlapping another family need no extra tunnel, and each pair receives at most one explicit link. No link is added if another family is out of range. These extra tunnels are additional to the Branch Density count; the entrance is excluded. Zero preserves previous layouts, while natural geometric intersections remain possible.

In Cave Generation Profile → Layout (or the component without a profile), Branch Density controls the number of side tunnels: zero disables them; one requests one per room. The total is rounded, and a one-room layout has no main passages to branch from. Enable Allow Nested Branches to let new branches start on earlier branches too. They remain connected to the cave; they can end blindly or intersect rooms and other passages.

Minimum Branch Length and Maximum Branch Length set straight-line branch reach in metres (2–64, default 7–12). Winding increases walked distance, and overlaps can shorten the visibly separate portion. Equal bounds give equal reach; reversed bounds raise maximum to minimum. Corridor Length Variation (0–1) adds seeded, randomly sized broad bends to room-to-room routes. It works independently of Corridor Winding, which adds smaller alternating bends. Main corridor lengths still depend on room positions; this is not an exact metre range. Extra Connections adds loops between rooms.

Try Branch Density 0.8, branch lengths 6–24 m, Allow Nested Branches enabled and Corridor Length Variation 0.7, then Rebuild Cave. Longer paths can increase rebuild cost and create new intersections. The entrance keeps its separate Entrance Length setting. New controls preserve the old layout by default: variation zero, nested branches disabled, branch reach 7–12 m.

Room size range

Room Spacing Multiplier (0.25–2, default 1) adjusts the distance between room centres without changing room radii. Try 0.5–0.7 for large halls with shorter connecting passages, then Rebuild Cave. One preserves the previous spacing; two doubles it. Low values can make chambers overlap and merge, so the number of visually distinct rooms can decrease. Main connection choices may change when the rooms move. Branch lengths and Entrance Length retain their own settings; reduce Corridor Length Variation and Corridor Winding if routes still take long detours.

In Cave Generation Profile → Layout, enable Use Room Radius Range, set Minimum Room Radius and Maximum Room Radius (2.5–32 metres), then use Rebuild Cave. Without a profile, use the same fields on Procedural Cave. For example, 4–16 produces small chambers and large halls. These are base radii: irregular wall shaping can extend beyond them. Equal bounds give equal base sizes; a single room uses the midpoint. Multiple rooms sample across the range and shuffle the sizes deterministically for the seed. Maximum is raised to minimum if the bounds are reversed.

Range mode replaces Room Radius and Room Size Variation. It starts disabled to preserve existing layouts; disable it to return to legacy sizing. Layout spacing uses the maximum radius in range mode. Large radii and many rooms increase mesh size and rebuild time. Population percentages and coins per room remain unchanged. Radius 16 gives four times the area of radius 8; radius 32 gives four times its diameter.

Damage camera shake

On the camera, Character Follow Camera → Damage Shake controls feedback for every accepted hit, including the fatal hit. Set Damage Shake Amplitude (0.18 metres), Damage Shake Duration (0.3 seconds) and Damage Shake Frequency (24). Zero amplitude or duration disables the effect. The camera reads health from its Target or its parent. This uses the existing shake system, including Shake Orbit Degrees; set that range to zero if no orbit change is wanted. Rapid hits refresh a bounded impulse instead of adding unlimited displacement. Rejected hits do not shake.

Darkness damage and injury sounds

Select Shadow Knight → Character Darkness Damage. While holding a burned-out torch in full darkness, Roll Interval (5 seconds) rolls d6 against X-in-6 (default 1): results from 1 through X succeed. Thus X=3 gives 50%, X=0 disables damage and X=6 guarantees it. Success removes one health segment and adds hit stress. Full Darkness Exposure (0.0001) is the shared probe cutoff. Light, empty hands or a burning torch reset the timer; death and completed runs stop the hazard. Dropping the torch stops this specific hazard. Torch discovery can take up to Torch Refresh Interval (1 second). Pauses stop the timer; long frames never cause a burst of rolls. Damage does not force a knockdown.

Select Player Damage Audio → Character Damage Audio and populate Injury Clips and Death Clips. Both lists are intentionally empty. Each hit randomly chooses a valid clip and pitch between its minimum and maximum (0.9–1.1); each group also has its own volume. Fatal hits play only the death sound, interrupting any injury sound. Empty/null entries are safe. Test nonlethal hits, fatal hits, returning to light before the next roll, and dropping an exhausted torch.

Breathing and darkness stress

Select Player Breathing in ProceduralCave to tune CharacterBreathingAudio: Volume sets loudness; Minimum Playback Speed (1) and Maximum Playback Speed (1.6) map stress 0–100 to breathing speed and pitch. Pitch Response Time smooths changes; Fade In Duration softens startup. The assigned Breath.wav loops continuously. Running Playback Multiplier (1.3) multiplies the stress-driven rate during actual running: maximum stress at 1.6 becomes 2.08. Standing still or stopping against a wall removes the multiplier smoothly. The final speed/pitch is capped at 3. Character defaults to the movement component on the assigned stress object.

On Shadow Knight, PlayerLightExposure supplies a shared probe for stress and whispers. Tune Sample Offset, Fully Lit Exposure (3), and Environment Mask here. When this shared probe is assigned, the whisper component’s legacy probe settings are unused. In the assigned player balance profile, Stress / Darkness Stress Per Second (5) controls stress gain at complete darkness; partial darkness scales it proportionally. A value of zero disables this gain. Returning to light stops the gain, while existing Recovery Per Second and Recovery Delay control recovery; zero recovery retains accumulated stress. Darkness also delays recovery. Check darkness, partial light, return to light, and stress-driven breathing speed.

Darkness whispers

In ProceduralCave, select Darkness Whispers to tune DarknessWhisperAudio. The whisper track loops from startup at zero volume, then fades towards maximum as local light disappears. Other nearby torches also suppress it. Maximum Volume sets the darkness ceiling (1); Fully Lit Exposure on the assigned player probe sets the illumination needed for silence (3); Fade Duration controls a full-range transition (3 seconds). Assign Player and use Sample Offset for the probe position. Environment Mask selects light-blocking colliders. This uses point/spot gameplay light and shadows, ignoring ambient/directional moonlight and screen effects. Mixer routing can be set on the dedicated AudioSource. Test torch burnout, moving away from a dropped torch, and returning to light.

01 / Orientation

Start with a playable question

ProceduralCave is the primary integration scene: generated cave, player, torch, residents, bodies, treasures, boulders, exit and inventory. TorchNight is a dark encounter course with authored centipedes. ProceduralMovement is the movement and gait playground. SampleScene is the original scaffold, not the reference for cave content.

  1. Open the relevant scene and inspect existing overrides before changing a prefab or a generator setting.
  2. Change one family of values at a time; regenerate procedural content from the ProceduralCave Inspector rather than editing its generated children.
  3. Enter Play Mode and test the transition that the change could break: blocked movement, range loss, light/shadow response, attack release, or exit/defeat.
  4. Keep a seed when judging a change. A new seed changes the question as well as the answer.
Vocabulary: a source default is the initializer in C#; an Inspector value is a prefab or scene serialization override; a runtime value can be changed again by profiles or generated setup. The catalog reports source defaults only. Inspect the selected object to see the effective saved value.

Inspector workflow

Select a profile asset, set Target root, then use Capture Local Fallback or Assign Profile in Edit Mode. Every related component also offers Create Balance Profile From Local Fallback. Player assignment is atomic across its seven required components; a missing component changes nothing, while replacing another profile reports a warning. Inspector workflow: select a balance profile asset, choose its Target root, then use Capture Local Fallback or Assign Profile. The action is disabled in Play Mode. Every player, torch and centipede balance component also exposes Create Balance Profile From Local Fallback in its component context menu; it captures local values, saves a new asset and assigns it.

Camera orbit angle

On Main Camera → CharacterFollowCamera, adjust Orbit Angle from -180 to 180 degrees to place the camera around the character. Zero keeps the original Offset; ±90 gives side views and ±180 the opposite side. The destination retains the offset height and distance. Rotation uses the world vertical axis and does not follow character facing. Smoothing controls travel to the new position; the camera aim turns with the angle. Rockfall shake remains layered over this view. Shake Orbit Degrees sets the minimum/maximum extra rotation per shake (default 2–5 degrees, with a random left/right direction). Shake Orbit Smooth Time controls the gradual turn (default 0.4 s). Each accepted shake, including first boulder landings, adds a step to the pending destination without snapping. The new viewing angle remains after shaking ends; it is runtime state added to the authored Orbit Angle. Set the range to 0/0 to stop adding turns. Disabling the component cancels the pending turn at its current angle. Tune in Play Mode to preview, then apply the chosen value in Edit Mode to save it.

Centipede impact

An accepted centipede hit drops the torch at the contact location, then pushes the player 2 m horizontally away from the attacker over 0.25 seconds while falling to their knees. Walls and rocks can shorten or deflect the shove through the existing character collision and step rules. There is no ledge protection: the player can be pushed into a Pit. Forced travel does not advance knee recovery, and rejected hits during immunity do not push again. Other knockdown sources keep their existing behavior. The shove uses fixed ImpactDistance and ImpactDuration constants in CharacterKnockdown; existing player profile settings still control posture and recovery.

05 / Safe extensions

Extend contracts, not generated children

Add a treasure variant

  1. Duplicate a treasure prefab and preserve its Treasure component, colliders and dither-capable materials.
  2. Duplicate or create a TreasureDefinition in Assets/Settings/Gameplay/Treasures/; assign it to the prefab. The definition owns display name, value and coin category. Use English display names: all player-facing UI, interaction prompts, inventory and expedition/defeat summaries are in English.
  3. Use SetValue only for per-instance runtime overrides. It never changes the shared definition.
  4. Add the prefab to treasureVariants, rebuild, then test illumination, blocked reach and inventory grouping.

Add generic cave content

  1. Create a CaveRoomContentRule with a stable seedSalt and prefab bag. It runs after legacy populations on an independent stream.
  2. Use the planar footprint, wall clearance and route clearance deliberately; route clearance 0 is for mobile/ambient content.
  3. Each selected room requests 0–64 instances and each has 128 attempts. Placement does not inspect colliders or navigation.
  4. Use ICaveSpawnParticipant for setup; its callback runs before activation. Exceptions remove the faulty instance.

Generic cave-rule limits: each selected room requests 0–64 instances; each instance receives at most 128 placement attempts. CaveGenerationProfile.Settings is snapshotted when Rebuild starts, so an edited asset takes effect only after a rebuild. ICaveSpawnParticipant.OnCaveSpawned(CaveSpawnContext) runs before the generated group activates and receives cave, rule, player, room index, instance index and seed. A callback exception is logged and its faulty instance is removed.

06 / Guardrails

Things that look editable but are contracts

  • Treasure identity: assign a TreasureDefinition for durable display name, unit value and coin category. It overrides legacy prefab-name/value data; SetValue is an instance-only runtime override.
  • Pose ordering: interaction or attack code must update suppression/weight every frame. A stale overlay leaves the torch or arm posed incorrectly.
  • Interaction priority: reachable treasure wins, then a free torch with empty hands, then a settled boulder, then exit. E never drops or replaces a held torch.
  • Forgiving pickup: treasure, torches and settled boulders transfer after the reach animation when still available, in range and unobstructed. Facing and exact hand IK contact do not gate success. Tune Pickup Range and Pickup Duration in the player balance profile; boulder range includes its radius. Hold E to lift/carry a boulder, release to drop it.
  • Reach checks twice: selection and transfer both validate range/activity/obstruction. Do not bypass the transfer check for a new pickup.
  • Lighting is not pixels: creature and treasure illumination is collider-based sampling. Transparent render appearance, baked light and dither tone are not authoritative gameplay input.
  • Rig names and lengths: this is not a Humanoid Avatar. Renaming limb nodes or changing them without proportions setup breaks cached procedural assumptions.
  • UI: project game UI uses TMP only. Add source UI under the source UI Canvas, not under the output RawImage canvas.

Open pits and cracked ground

Pits are authored as prefabs in Room Content Rules: the playable profile contains Open Pit.prefab and Cracked Pit.prefab entries (Content Type = Prefabs), each currently selecting 25% of rooms. Edit Assets/Prefabs/Open Pit.prefab or Assets/Prefabs/Cracked Pit.prefab, then rebuild the cave. Rule frequency, count and Seed Salt control deterministic population; legacy standalone Pit Traps profile settings are retained only as hidden migration data.

The prefab's CavePitTrap component controls Minimum Size, Maximum Size, Irregularity, Depth and Collapse Seconds. The rule reserves at least Maximum Size / 2 + 0.3 m, ignores wall placement and surface offset, keeps the prefab upright, requires at least Route Clearance 1 m and Wall Clearance 0.6 m, and excludes entrance and exit rooms. Small or crowded rooms may yield fewer instances.

Pits remain physical holes in the floor and collider. Cracked cover supports the player until 25% of its selected collapse duration, then releases fragments; leaving does not reset it. Falling 0.8 m below the rim is lethal regardless of remaining health or knockdown immunity, while a completed run remains protected. Tune source prefabs and rules, never generated pit children.

Rockfall camera and sound

The current authored cave profile uses pebble sizes of 0.1–0.2 m. Torch Cave uses brightness 7 and range 18 m; Player Default uses two health slots and 20 seconds per refill. The Shadow Knight prefab uses upper-arm/forearm lengths of 0.468/0.444 m and torso height 0.735 m. These are asset overrides, separate from the source defaults in the catalog.

On the persistent ProceduralCave object, use CaveRockfallFeedback to tune all generated rockfalls. These presentation settings are scene-local; rebuilding preserves them. Do not edit generated CaveBoulder objects.

Assign Collapse Clips and optional mixer Output. One randomly selected non-empty clip plays once when the warning turns into a collapse, at the middle of the trap. Volume, Pitch Range (default 0.85–1.15), and Audio Min/Max Distance control its sound. Every collapse gets an independently randomized pitch without changing seeded rock placement.

Shake Enabled switches shaking off. Collapse Amplitude and Landing Amplitude set displacement in metres; Shake Duration and Shake Frequency set the decaying impulse. Strength is full within Shake Full Distance of the player and fades to zero at Shake Max Distance. Assign Follow Camera explicitly or use the main camera with CharacterFollowCamera. Simultaneous landings refresh rather than sum impulses. Only the first landing shakes; later carrying and dropping do not. An empty list (or a list containing only empty entries) suppresses audio without disabling shake.

Character focus: Circle and Tilt Shift

Select Main Camera > Character Depth Of Field. Mode switches between Circle, Tilt Shift and the previous Background Depth. The center follows Target; use Center Offset to shift it toward the torso. Positive Y moves up. Preview changes in Game view, including Edit Mode.

Circle: Sharp Radius sets the crisp disk and Circle Transition sets the soft outer ring. The default radius is 0.14 and transition is 0.16. Tilt Shift: zero Tilt Angle makes a horizontal band through the character; positive angles rise to the right. Upper Sharp Width and Lower Sharp Width control the sharp area on each side (default 0.08 each); Upper Transition and Lower Transition independently set the fade to full blur (default 0.18). All these screen sizes are fractions of viewport height: 0.1 means 10%. Aspect correction keeps the disk circular in wide or tall windows.

Blur Strength sets the Gaussian radius, from 0 (off) to 1.5; default 1.2. The two screen-space modes ignore object depth, so the top, bottom and sides can blur. Background Depth retains Sharp Margin (2 m) and Blur Range (6 m), only blurring surfaces behind the character. The assigned Global Volume must contain Depth Of Field; leave the custom PostProcessData on PC/Mobile renderers. Disable the component to restore authored Volume settings. UI rendered separately stays sharp. Review the project Gaussian shader when upgrading URP.

Dithering and Volume effects

The renderer gathers accent masks, applies OneBitDither, then runs URP post-processing. Enable Post Processing on the world camera. Add a Global Volume with an assigned profile, Weight = 1 and a layer included by the camera's Volume Mask. Enable each effect and its parameter overrides. Bloom, blur and color grading affect the already dithered image, so they can soften the pattern and change the final palette. Keep the old Full Screen Pass disabled.

Living shadows near a dying torch

In Play Mode, dark fingers extend from visible dark regions toward the character holding a torch. A configurable set of noise-bent directions slowly extends and retracts. Remaining fuel increases reach and width; the final 2.5% fades the effect into darkness. Dropping or disabling the held torch removes the effect; picking one up uses its remaining fuel. This is visual only and does not change enemy exposure, damage or torch consumption.

Open Assets/Settings/OneBitDither.mat. Living Shadows enables the effect; Shadow Reach At Low Fuel defaults to 3.5 metres, Shadow Tendril Width to 0.55, Shadow Motion Speed to 0.45 and Shadow Player Safe Radius to 0.85 metres (plus a 0.65 m transition). Set speed to zero for a stationary pattern. Shadow Count In Bright Light (default 4) and Shadow Count In Dim Light (default 12) accept 1–24. Count increases with the same burnout envelope that dims the torch, ignoring rapid flicker. The dim count is clamped to at least the bright count; equal values keep count fixed. New fingers grow along fixed directions without relocating existing ones. Their visible number depends on nearby dark regions. This follows torch dimming, not measured illuminated floor area or other lights. More fingers add bounded shader arithmetic; the four image samples remain unchanged. The dark core is applied before dithering for a readable silhouette, while the existing player mask and camera UI rectangle protect interaction readability.

The shape uses world positions reconstructed from depth, but its dark-region attachment uses four screen-space color samples. Off-screen darkness and background cannot seed fingers. Samples are checked against their expected world position: confidence fades between 0.08 and 0.35 metres of error, and a rejected sample stops farther taps. This prevents foreground rocks or distant walls from projecting their silhouettes onto another surface. Steep slopes can suppress fingers; dark regions on a continuous surface can still seed them. The depth rejection fix compiled without shader errors and was inspected at two camera positions 1.5 metres apart in paused Play Mode; this was a camera-position check, not a physical movement-input test. The original camera position and paused Play Mode were preserved. Other lights affect the sampled image, not an exact light-volume boundary. There are no new render targets, lights, physics or per-frame object searches. GPU cost still needs measurement on the target hardware.

Verified in ProceduralCave: C# and shader compilation, camera captures at full and approximately 14% fuel (holding illumination fixed to isolate the mask), and direct fuel/disable/re-enable/burnout/drop calls. These are not physical input tests or GPU profiling. Test state was discarded on leaving Play Mode.

The current authored cave uses seed 2 and 17 rooms. Existing scene overrides preserved with this change include knight proportions, camera orbit shake, six collapse clips, a 1-second shake duration and 30-metre maximum shake distance; manually placed chalice and coin examples are inactive.

08 / Candid architecture notes

Current limits worth designing around

  • Balance is shareable through typed profiles, while visual and scene-specific references remain local.
  • Cave meshes and generated children are disposable; authored details below them vanish at rebuild.
  • Centipede navigation is local surface steering; topology and collider quality can still stall it.
  • Gameplay light exposure is collider-based and intentionally differs from rendered/baked light.
  • Inventory and torch fuel have no persistent save system. Execution-order overlays remain coupled: torch 100, attack 150, interaction 175.
